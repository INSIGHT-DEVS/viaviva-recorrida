04The story
The story

The track that once divided, now connects.

Until 2019, the train ran at street leveland split the neighbourhood in two.

With the Mitre Viaduct the tracks went up and the crossing gates disappeared.Below, the space where VIAVIVA stands today was freed up.

4 kmof elevated tracks

The train now runs overhead between Congreso and Dorrego avenues, across Núñez, Belgrano and Palermo.

8crossing gates removed

Monroe, Blanco Encalada, Mendoza, Juramento, Olazábal, Sucre, La Pampa and Olleros no longer have a gate. Four streets that were cut by the tracks were opened as well.

650,000people benefited

Everyone who crossed or lived next to the tracks: 100,000 daily passengers of the Tigre line, 30,000 bus riders, 70,000 drivers and 450,000 residents of Núñez, Belgrano and Palermo.

Up to 20 minless travel time per day

At peak hours the busiest gates in the city stayed down for about 20 minutes of every hour. The one at Vértiz and La Pampa, on this same line, up to 35. No gates, no waiting.

From empty ground to meeting point

The same space under the viaduct: first, dirt and weeds; today, the place to come, discover and enjoy.

La Pampa & the tracks

A Buenos Aires saying was born here

“Estar en Pampa y la vía” is what porteños say of someone who is flat broke. And it comes from this crossing.

Between 1887 and 1926 the Hipódromo Nacional racetrack operated near where River Plate's stadium stands today. A free tram took the crowds there, and its line ended at La Pampa and the tracks. That is where the gamblers who had lost everything at the races got off, and had to find their way home.

That gate was one of the ones the viaduct removed: today, at La Pampa, you walk under the train.
